# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Turn taking: turn segmentation, floor transfers, and entropy measures.
- Rule-based coding of backchannels, pauses, and interruptions.
- Inter-coder agreement report with Krippendorff's alpha and Cohen's kappa.
- Codebooks defined in YAML, with validation.
- Human review queue for low-confidence and abstained labels.
- Two-way ELAN (.eaf) interchange for annotations.
- Export to CSV, Parquet, ELAN, and fixed-rate state sequences.
- Command line interface for setting up a project, running extractors, and inspecting config.
- Body movement measures from pose, quality checks that point at the frames worth reviewing (`macaw qc --render`), your own choice and grouping of what runs (`pipeline:`), and a template for adding your own extractors (`macaw new-extractor`).
- Lining up files from separate devices by their sound (`macaw align`), with a figure per file to check the result, and setting an offset by hand (`macaw set-offset`).
- Pose from YOLO as well as MediaPipe, chosen explicitly (`pose.backend` has no default), runs that stop for a person only where something is in doubt (`macaw accept-offset`, `macaw identify`, `macaw accept-owner`), and usage reporting by the tools pymacaw calls turned off by default.
- People on a shared camera kept together when the tracker loses and finds them, matched to who they are by seat or by a person (`macaw accept-owner --rename`), an HTML quality report (`macaw qc --report`), and your own backends (`macaw new-backend`).
- Correcting who is who no longer reruns the pose model, resuming notices changed settings or inputs, the quality report draws each person over time, mics hint at who is who on a shared camera, and speech and prosody backend templates (`macaw new-backend --kind`).
- Motion energy per person from a hand-drawn region (`motion.mea`) with its own quality check and report figure, a shared camera read next to each person's own camera (`pose.read_shared`), which names its people by their movement and gives the distances between them, a check that a transcript belongs to the audio it is filed under, mic bleed judged fairly between mics recorded at different gains, every room camera read and named, talk exported as per-person and floor sequences, openSMILE feature sets per turn (`prosody.standard_sets`) and facial action units through py-feat (`face.au`), both installed by the user.
- Mouth movement, eye opening and blinks per person from the face landmarks (`face.movement`), gaze direction per frame from the face model (`face.gaze`), and a check that each person's mouth moves when they are the one talking.
- A voice report per turn (`prosody.voice_report`: pitch statistics and slope, jitter, shimmer, HNR, CPPS), lexical measures per speaker with optional sentence embeddings (`text.features`), a Methods section with its BibTeX for what a project ran (`macaw methods`), and a second face model to check the action units against (`face.check_model`, `au_agreement`); transcripts that reach past the recording are cut to it.
- A shared microphone (`text.diarization: pyannote`), alone or beside some people's own microphones: the voices are found by diarization, named by a person with a suggestion from their own microphone or mouth movement (`macaw accept-voices`), and then transcribed and measured for pitch and voice quality on each person's own stretches; the words counted for who brings in shared vocabulary are your choice (`text.first_use_*`).
- Every step that decides by itself can be checked or handed to a person: gates take `always`, `flagged` or `skip` (including the new `text.voice_review`), the quality report lists each decision and who made it, speech left out of a transcript is kept with the reason, and a transcript can be corrected in ELAN (`macaw transcript`); a workbench to run, review and browse results (`macaw gui`); syntax per speaker (`text.syntax`); and face landmarks from MediaPipe or OpenFace (`face.backend`).
- Speech acts coded by a language model from a codebook (`coding.judge`, `macaw code`), on your own model server or a cloud one, each unit asked several times so the agreement of the answers is its confidence; doubtful units go to a person (`macaw review --export / --apply`, or the Review page of `macaw gui`). A second face backend can check the action units of the first (`face.check_backend`), model and transcript folders written relative to project.yaml are found from there, and a blink rate is left empty when the face backend's eyelids never close.
- A redesigned `macaw gui`, light or dark: the Run page shows which steps are up to date, waiting for a person or due to run again, and a Settings page edits project.yaml as a form or as text, checking the settings and naming the steps they put out of date before saving. A project's roles must now match its number of people, and a language model setting no longer reruns the rule-based coding.
- Videos are prepared once before any video step (`prep.video`): constant frame rate, upright, square pixels, deinterlaced, with frame 0 at the file's first sound, so every step reads the same frames on the same clock; `video.fps` brings all videos to one rate where it divides them evenly, the quality report lists differing frame rates, and audio tracks from files whose sound and pictures start apart now land on the aligned clock.
- A recording filmed by one camera with no separate microphone is analysed from the camera's own sound, and `text.extra_voices` lets a shared microphone hold voices besides the participants (a researcher in the room) that are named `none` and left out. `segment.researcher_exit_marker`, which had no effect, is gone.
- With `text.extra_voices`, voices named `none` take part in turn taking as one further speaker, `other`, and stay out of the people's language and voice measures; high dynamic range video (HLG, PQ) is tone-mapped to standard range by `prep.video`. On a Slurm cluster, `macaw hpc generate` writes job arrays for what is left to run and `macaw hpc prefetch-models` fetches model weights beforehand; **`execution.backend` and `coding.llm.launch` are gone, the model-server lines now go in `hpc.yaml`**.
- A user guide for researchers, in plain words, published to GitHub Pages (`docs/`, built with `pip install ".[docs]"`).
- Language-model coding shows the talk of people not being coded, and of other voices such as a researcher, in place as context, and `coding.llm.coded_roles` codes only the roles named (for example only the child); the quality report draws other voices on its timeline and saves its figures at 300 dpi. The empty `macaw refine-codebook` command is gone. A run fetches any missing model weights before it starts and its steps then run offline, as cluster jobs do, so a model never changes or downloads halfway through.
- Your own body measures between points on people (`kinematics.measures`), and a `scripts` folder in the project whose Python files MACAW uses without installing (measure functions, steps, backends); on a shared microphone, speech is recognised before the voices are named, so the suggestion can also use patterns you write for what each role says and the order of their pitch (`identity.voice_hints`, `identity.pitch_order`); an overview of talk, movement, distance and codes at the top of the quality report's Over time section; `semantic_following` between speakers from the sentence embeddings; and the octave check flags a pitch track that sits an octave high throughout (`qc.f0_median_max_by_role`).
- Kinematics of each gesture you coded (`kinematics.gestures`, step `pose.gestures`): peak speed, submovements, holds, rhythm, area, height and gesture space per hand; `macaw describe-codes` for how each code was used, its runs and how many last one unit; entropy windows count their turn pairs and warn when they hold too few; and the voices on a shared microphone show their pitch, their longest lines and the evidence behind the suggestion, in the quality report and on the review page.
- Centimetres beside pixels in exports, from an object of known length clicked on a frame of the recording or of a calibration clip filmed beforehand (`macaw set-scale`, or the Review page) or from `scale.cm_per_px`, a scale for each person's place, and a check of each person's size at their scale; 3D in metres from two or more cameras calibrated with a printed board (`macaw calibration-board`, `macaw calibrate-cameras`, `pose3d`) or an Anipose file, with 3D measures and a 3D view; gestures also count the directions they reach out in and can count holds only while both hands are still (`kinematics.gestures.holds`); `macaw qc` uses each recording's own settings.
- In 3D, your own body measures (`<name>_3d`; a script function with `in_3d: true`) and the kinematics of each coded gesture (`pose.gestures3d`, with `kinematics.gestures.still_speed_3d` and `peak_prominence_3d` in m/s, height and gesture space in each person's own frontal plane); a Cameras page in the workbench to print the board, find the cameras, check them and choose the setup. Triangulation no longer places a point where the views left disagree or that falls behind a camera, and the 3D views keep people upright whatever axes a calibration file uses.
- `macaw export -f skeleton`: each recording's body points as a CSV, a row per person and frame, with a second file in metres for 3D; `scale.size_window_s` for the size check when people walk about; yes-or-no settings and a Video card on the settings form. Head yaw and pitch say which way is positive; with several cameras a person counts as missing only while no camera sees them.
- The workbench opens on an Overview page (counts, what to do next, where each recording and step stands), each step on the Run page has its own Run, and Open folder opens the project folder. A YOLO or OpenFace window late in a video is cut without decoding the file from the start, and face.au with py-feat holds about 3 GB less in memory.
- Who looks at whom (`gaze.target`): Gaze-LLE finds the point each person looks at from the picture, a head box from the body points names whom it lands on, things named in `gaze.targets` are found by Grounding DINO, and `gaze.backend: angles` reads head and eye angles against `gaze.seats` or labelled frames instead; `looks_at`, `mutual_gaze` and their shares, codes for ELAN; labelling frames on the Review page (`macaw label-gaze`) and a `gaze_labels` check of agreement. A resumed run no longer redoes recordings that have their own settings file, and the `scale` check runs by default as it was meant to.
- Your own coding comes in beside the machine's (`macaw import-codes`, or the Review page) from ELAN or a CSV, TSV or Excel table, with `coding.imports` saying whose each tier is, its name and its codes; `macaw agreement --units` compares on one coder's intervals, so a coder's freely drawn codes meet the language model's utterances; coded gestures are read from it. A look shorter than `gaze.min_look_s` (0.2 s) joins the one before it. A run stops when a recording's own settings file needs a step the project does not run, the angles backend of `gaze.target` runs without labelled frames, and `macaw clean --all` says it deletes what people did by hand too.
- `macaw agreement` gives each code a kappa for every pair of coders, so a language model's agreement with each person sits beside the people's own, and `--out` writes `report.json` and `units.csv` (a row per unit, a column per coder) into a folder. `macaw clean --all` now keeps what people did by hand and deletes only the computed output, and the Review page takes *Your name* as typed when a file is dropped on *Import your coding*.
- Several codings by the language model side by side (`coding.judges`), each changing only its codebook or model settings and kept under its own name, so codebook versions or models can be compared with each other and with people; `coding.llm.show` chooses what the model sees of each line: its timing, its pitch, loudness and speech rate against the speaker's own, or the words only. An answer cut off at `coding.llm.max_tokens` (now 8192) before any code stops the step and says so instead of counting as "cannot tell", and the run plan lists every coding's requests and cloud notice.
- More ways to set what the language model is told, to see how much it needs: a codebook of code names alone, a note in your own words (`coding.llm.notes`), units people coded as examples (`macaw draw-examples` writes a table to edit, `coding.llm.examples` shows it, never a recording's own), a few words on what each code rests on (`coding.llm.reasons`), reasoning before the answer on or off on a local server (`coding.llm.think`), and three video frames per unit for a model that reads pictures (`show: [frames]`). `macaw agreement --samples` counts each of the model's answers as a coder, so a difference between versions can be set against the model's own variation, and `units.csv` carries each unit's words and the line before. The review queue also takes a few units the model was sure of, drawn at random (`coding.llm.review_random`), and once people decide them the QC report says how often its sure answers were wrong. A model server on the same machine is reached at 127.0.0.1 and asked its version once, which on Windows had slowed every request, and frames past the end of a video are left out instead of stopping the step.
- Camcorder files (.MTS transport streams) are read at the right moment: the prepared copy of one whose pictures start after its sound keeps that gap, and frames are found in one by reading up to them, as a jump in such a file lands late. Single frames, for the language model and the labelling pages, are the nearest one to the time asked, counted from the first picture.
- The user guide is rewritten around using MACAW: the standard procedure on one page and the optional features on another, now with a codebook example, a local language model server, the backchannel word list and motion-energy regions, which it had not explained; running on a cluster moved there from the README.
- Named segments of a recording, such as the phases of a task, each get every recording-level measure again, worked out as if the recording had been cut at the segment's edges while transcription, voices and tracking keep the whole recording (`segments.csv` next to project.yaml, `macaw set-segment`, or the new Segments page, which plays the recording, marks a part where it is playing and can also set the analysed part); moving a segment reruns only its summaries (`segments.summary`); exports carry a `segment` column, one row per person for the whole recording and one for each segment, and the QC report, the Results page and the Methods text show them. The QC report now shows track swaps, which it had looked for under the wrong file name, a recording's summaries no longer split over several rows of a wide export, and a local run reports its cache hits.
- `macaw agreement --segment` compares coders within one named segment of each recording, cut at its edges, which also serves when a coder coded only part of a recording. On a time grid or one coder's units, a hair of overlap left by rounding no longer counts as coverage nor decides an even split, and the Segments page passes long recordings' times in full.
- A model that reads pictures can check the review frames (`macaw qc --look`, `qc.look`): it counts the people in view and says of each person drawn whether it is a person and, for the participants described in `qc.look.people`, which one, so that frames with someone missed, someone drawn twice, a skeleton on no one or a name on the wrong person are flagged; a person marks it right or wrong on those frames and on a few it passed, drawn at random (`macaw check-look`, the Review page), and the QC report and Methods text give both.
- An Agreement page in the workbench compares coders on one tier across the recordings; the turn checks are made again within each named segment and listed under it in the quality report; and a square on the Overview opens the step's results, the recording's reviews or the Run page with that recording and step selected.
- Each recording folder now holds one folder per camera or microphone, described once on a new Setup page (whom each camera is read for, for body, face and gaze, and whose voices each device carries); nothing is read from file names, and nothing runs before the setup is saved. Who is who on a camera is clicked by a person (`macaw name-people`) and followed by MACAW; suggestions from head motion or a person's own microphone are optional, as are suggested voices on a shared microphone.
- The Setup page also lays out the room (people, cameras, microphones and things in three views and 3D, with notes on what each camera sees, a camera's view drawn over its own frame, and `macaw draw-room` for a paper), names people on each camera by clicking on a frame, and holds the 3D camera calibration; gaze from head direction reads positions from the room. Optional name suggestions now include the person most in view on a single-person camera and a model that reads pictures. One length unit per project (centimetres, metres or inches) for everything typed or exported.
- The Results page and the cameras for three dimensions show lengths in the project's unit with each measure's unit named, and the QC report, the Methods text and the room card follow the unit wherever they still assumed centimetres or metres.
- The Setup page is one editor for the whole setup: the room's size first, a list of people, devices and things pinned on a plan or dropped into a view, the room in 3D with handles to move, turn, tilt and raise them, three flat views drawn for a paper or as the model, the chosen one's settings with what a camera sees, and the room seen from a camera over a frame of its file. The workbench fills the window's width.
- The workbench comes in three styles chosen on the settings page for each browser (Lab, the default, white with a teal accent; Playroom, warm with a wooden room; Blueprint, navy on gridded paper), their fonts shipped with it, one dark palette over any of them; the Setup page's 3D view keeps the whole room in view when it is narrow.
- The Setup page places calibrated cameras in its room from the calibration (From the calibration, or macaw place-cameras), tied to the room by whatever was measured there (camera places, heights, facings, tilts) and by the board lying flat on the floor or a table at a moment of the board clips (Board lying flat, or macaw calibration-floor); what is still missing is named and values that do not agree are marked; a camera tilt left blank is no longer taken for level.
- The workbench opens straight in the style and light or dark chosen, its pages explain themselves in one short sentence each, and in the plan a name too long for its circle sits behind the person, clear of the facing arrow.
- The language model proposes a clearer codebook from the units a person and the model coded differently (macaw codebook refine, or the Agreement page), drafts a first one from the transcripts (macaw codebook draft), and marks transcript lines that look misheard for a person to check (macaw transcript check, or the Review page), never changing the codebook in use or the transcript.
- The Methods text says for how many recordings a transcript corrected by hand replaced the recognised one.
- Joint attention to a named object with who led and how long the other took to follow, each turn's voice measures set beside the previous speaker's at a change of speaker, lexical overlap with the previous turn, time a measure spends below a value the study sets, body orientation toward the other person in 3D, and guide examples for rMEA, SUSY and response rates within a window.
- The workbench asks for the reviewer's name instead of taking the login name, keeps imports on the cards they belong to, counts the speech acts decided, links the sections of its long pages and shows short settings notes; the README and the guide show screenshots of it.
- 3D from a depth camera (`depth: true` in the setup, `macaw depth-import` for RealSense, Azure Kinect and Orbbec recordings) or, as an option, from one camera by a body model, with people's heights from `heights.csv`; cameras placed from where the flat board lay alone; the transcript check tells misheard lines from cut-off pieces; checks on speech and tracking within each segment.
